# Teacher's Manual - Pedagogical Context

## Purpose of This File

Use this file when working on the `/manual` page or any teacher-facing description of the platform. This is the main internal reference for pedagogical framing, classroom use, grade recommendations, skills, and school-content positioning.

This file is about teacher-facing context, not low-level game rules. Detailed mechanics should stay in each game's own rule file or rules page.

## Core Educational Objective

The "Jogando com Lógica" platform serves as an interactive auxiliary tool for mathematics and logic educators. The games are specifically tailored to let students practice arithmetic, spatial planning, working memory, and rapid problem-solving while feeling completely immersed in play, rather than taking a traditional "test".

## Important Positioning Notes

- The platform is meant to support teachers, not replace them
- It should be presented as a practical classroom resource, not as a complete curriculum
- Grade ranges and pedagogical fit are recommendations based on current judgment, not fully validated conclusions
- The project does not currently have validated ongoing school adoption, so teacher-facing writing should not overclaim proven classroom impact

## Classroom Application

- **Zero Friction:** Teachers do not need to register their students, create classes, or manage passwords. Students simply open the link and start playing immediately.
- **Format:** The platform is versatile. It can be used individually on mobile phones/tablets or collaboratively in pairs on school computers.
- **Timeframe:** Perfect for filling standard 40-50 minute school lab sessions with engaging, productive activities.
- **Teacher role:** The games can be used as reinforcement, warm-up, practice, rotation-station activity, or after-school enrichment.

## Targeted Competencies

1. **Mental Math (Aritmética Mental):** Rapid calculation under pressure (for example, *Stop Matemático*, *Caça Soma*).
2. **Spatial Logic (Lógica Espacial):** Thinking ahead, predicting opponent moves, and understanding grid relationships (for example, *Caça Coroa*, *Guerra Matemática*, *Super Jogo da Velha*).
3. **Problem Solving (Resolução de Problemas):** Breaking down complex situations into logical steps (for example, *Cubo Mágico*).
4. **Resilience:** Learning to lose, iterate, and try different strategies without frustration.
5. **Attention and focus:** Sustaining concentration under time pressure or strategic constraints.

## Teacher Needs the Manual Should Cover

When writing or updating the manual page, make sure it answers the questions a teacher actually has:

- What is this game?
- What grades is it probably best for?
- What prior knowledge helps?
- What abilities or school content does it practice?
- How long does it usually take?
- Is it better for individual use, pairs, or whole-class rotation?
- How much explanation or mediation does the teacher need to give?
- What are the current limitations?

## Broad BNCC / School Content Framing

The manual does not need rigid BNCC code mapping everywhere, but it can reference broad school-content areas when useful.

Common content areas the platform may support:

- Basic operations
- Mental calculation
- Number decomposition and composition
- Multiplication and division reasoning
- Pattern recognition
- Logical sequencing
- Spatial reasoning and location on grids
- Strategy, anticipation, and decision-making
- Area, volume, and geometric visualization in cube-based activities

These should be presented as broad possible alignments, not as formal certification or exhaustive BNCC coverage.

## Current Teacher-Facing Summary by Game

### Stop Matemático

**What it is:** A fast arithmetic game inspired by traditional Stop, but using calculations instead of words. Each round generates a Magic Number, and the student solves addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division operations mentally under time pressure.

**Suggested grade range:** Best current fit is around 3rd to 7th grade, depending on level and teacher support.

**Prior knowledge that helps:**

- Basic addition
- Basic subtraction
- Growing familiarity with multiplication and division in later levels

**Abilities and content worked:**

- Rapid mental calculation
- Basic operations
- Attention under time pressure
- Number sense and flexible calculation

**Possible school-content / BNCC-aligned themes:**

- Mental math fluency
- Operations with natural numbers
- Strategy for choosing efficient calculations

**Estimated use in class:**

- Short independent rounds
- Warm-up activity
- Rotation station
- Individual reinforcement

**Approximate time:** Roughly 1 to 5 minutes per round.

**Teacher mediation notes:**

- Works well when the teacher wants fast arithmetic practice without paper
- Different students may need different levels of tolerance for time pressure
- It can be framed as repetition with challenge, not just speed

**Current limitations:**

- Currently solo-focused
- Multiplayer is a future idea, not current reality

### Caça Soma

**What it is:** A grid puzzle game where the student finds numbers that add up to a target Magic Number. It includes solo levels, legacy same-device versus play, and server-authoritative online private rooms for 1v1 or collaborative 2v2 races.

**Suggested grade range:** Best current fit is around 4th to 7th grade.

**Prior knowledge that helps:**

- Addition
- Comfort with combining multiple numbers
- Basic decomposition of values

**Abilities and content worked:**

- Rapid addition
- Number decomposition
- Visual scanning
- Accuracy under time pressure
- Team coordination in online 2v2

**Possible school-content / BNCC-aligned themes:**

- Addition strategies
- Composition and decomposition of numbers
- Mental calculation
- Efficient search and verification

**Estimated use in class:**

- Individual challenge
- Pair comparison
- Online 1v1 race
- Collaborative online 2v2 activity
- Timed station activity

**Approximate time:** Around 1 to 4 minutes in level mode, or longer in versus and online matches.

**Teacher mediation notes:**

- Good for showing that there can be multiple valid combinations for the same target
- Works best when students are encouraged to explain the logic they used after a round
- Online 2v2 can be used when the teacher wants students to discuss number decomposition with a partner

**Current limitations:**

- Grade recommendation is still provisional
- Best classroom use pattern still needs more real-world validation
- Online rooms currently require a short room code; classroom room discovery is a future idea

### Cubo Mágico

**What it is:** Guided classes and challenges using Rubik's cubes of different sizes to explore mathematical ideas in a visual and concrete way.

**Suggested grade range:** Best current fit is around 3rd to 6th grade, with different modules for different grades.

**Prior knowledge that helps:**

- Counting
- Multiplication basics
- Visual reasoning
- Comfort following guided steps

**Abilities and content worked:**

- Spatial reasoning
- Counting of visible units
- Area and volume intuition
- Pattern recognition
- Problem solving

**Possible school-content / BNCC-aligned themes:**

- Geometric visualization
- Area and volume ideas
- Multiplication through structured grouping
- Logical procedures and sequencing

**Estimated use in class:**

- Guided teacher-led activity
- Enrichment module
- Small-group exploration

**Approximate time:** Varies more by module than by round; better treated as a guided class block than a quick game.

**Teacher mediation notes:**

- This is more lesson-like than the other games
- It benefits from stronger teacher framing and explanation
- It can connect abstract math ideas to a concrete object students can manipulate

**Current limitations:**

- Only part of the planned module sequence is complete
- Current content is still limited compared to the long-term vision

### Super Jogo da Velha

**What it is:** A more advanced version of tic-tac-toe with 9 boards, where each move determines which small board the next player must play on. To win, the player must capture 3 small boards in a line on the larger board.

**Suggested grade range:** Best current fit is around 5th to 7th grade.

**Prior knowledge that helps:**

- Familiarity with normal tic-tac-toe
- Ability to think one or more moves ahead

**Abilities and content worked:**

- Strategic planning
- Anticipation of moves
- Logical reasoning
- Management of local and global objectives at the same time

**Possible school-content / BNCC-aligned themes:**

- Logical strategy
- Pattern recognition
- Multi-step planning
- Decision-making under constraints

**Estimated use in class:**

- Pairs
- Tournament bracket
- Strategy discussion activity

**Approximate time:** Around 10 to 30 minutes.

**Teacher mediation notes:**

- Useful for students who are ready for deeper planning games
- Encourages discussion of short-term and long-term strategy
- The AI mode can support individual practice

**Current limitations:**

- Better for students already comfortable with more abstract planning
- May require a short demonstration before independent play

### Caça Coroa

**What it is:** A strategy board game similar to chess, but simpler and faster, on a 5x5 board. The objective is to capture the enemy King, which does not move. Players use two piece types with different movement rules.

**Suggested grade range:** Best current fit is around 3rd to 7th grade, depending on the class and teacher support.

**Prior knowledge that helps:**

- Understanding of turn-based board games
- Willingness to think ahead and compare options

**Abilities and content worked:**

- Strategic planning
- Anticipation of moves
- Logical and tactical reasoning

**Possible school-content / BNCC-aligned themes:**

- Strategy and planning
- Grid-based spatial reasoning
- Logical consequences of choices

**Estimated use in class:**

- Pairs
- Small tournaments
- Strategy practice station

**Approximate time:** Around 5 to 20 minutes.

**Teacher mediation notes:**

- Good entry point for strategic board-game thinking because it is simpler than chess
- Teachers can ask students to justify why a move is safe, risky, defensive, or aggressive

**Current limitations:**

- Recommended grades are still a practical estimate
- Some classes may need direct demonstration before play begins

### Guerra Matemática

**What it is:** A board game inspired by chess that combines strategy with arithmetic. Each piece has a value, and movement depends on dice result plus piece value, generating energy that is spent on moves and captures.

**Suggested grade range:** Best current fit is around 6th to 7th grade.

**Prior knowledge that helps:**

- Comfort with addition and quick mental calculation
- Readiness for more complex strategic games

**Abilities and content worked:**

- Rapid mental calculation
- Strategy and planning
- Tactical reasoning
- Resource management through piece energy

**Possible school-content / BNCC-aligned themes:**

- Arithmetic with natural numbers
- Strategic planning
- Multi-step decision-making
- Quantitative reasoning linked to game actions

**Estimated use in class:**

- Pairs
- Advanced logic group
- Teacher-guided challenge round

**Approximate time:** Around 10 to 30 minutes.

**Teacher mediation notes:**

- One of the most cognitively demanding current games
- Better after students understand the rules well
- Can be used with older or more advanced groups for a deeper mix of math and strategy

**Current limitations:**

- More complex than the rest of the catalog
- Probably needs clearer initial explanation than simpler games

## General Writing Guidance for the Manual Page

Teacher-facing writing should:

- Use practical and direct language
- Explain value without sounding exaggerated
- Avoid presenting recommendations as scientific certainty
- Make classroom use feel easy and realistic
- Emphasize zero-friction access and low setup time
- Keep detailed game mechanics in the rules pages, not overloaded into the manual
