## Origins in 2024

### Motivation and first contact

Bruno initially sought a project entirely because of study-abroad applications. Daqui Pra Fora, which advised him on those applications, emphasized that he needed a substantial project for a good profile. He had always liked math. Teaching came to mind quickly, partly through a suggestion from his mother that he remembers as indirect rather than explicit.

He liked Rubik's cubes and had already taught many people to solve them. Cubes seemed like something distinctive he could bring to an educational project.

His mother knew the director of a municipal school in Novo Hamburgo, Rio Grande do Sul. Bruno went there without a name or settled plan. The broad idea was teaching math with cubes alongside interesting games and playful activities. He brought his own cubes from the first visit. The project was never intended to be exclusively about cubes.

### Classroom support and small groups

The historical notes describe initially helping a teacher in a contraturno group, outside students' regular school shift. Children from 3rd–5th grade shared the room; parents sought extra study time or somewhere for children to stay during those hours.

Bruno created plans, ran lessons, helped children with activities, and provided classroom support. Later he had his own space for groups of approximately 4–8 children.

### Chess and original activities

The school asked him to teach chess for an upcoming competition. The historical notes record teaching and practice, accompanying students to the competition, and some students winning medals. This was an early major activity, not evidence that cubes were absent before it.

He subsequently developed his own activities and games, often using chessboards because those were available. The project took a more recognizable form through that work.

### Later 2024 structure

The historical schedule describes continued support for the contraturno teacher alongside dedicated small-group sessions: three grade levels and three class periods, with groups rotating so more children could participate in later weeks. This continued to the end of 2024.

Rotation made repeating successful activities useful because the same children did not return frequently. Bruno completed high school in 2024.

## Expansion and formalization in 2025

### Organization

Bruno had more time in 2025 before college. He worked on expanding JcL and completing study-abroad applications. The older history describes a gap year before intended study abroad in 2026. That is a plan recorded at the time, not evidence that studying abroad subsequently happened.

Early in 2025 he established the name Jogando com Lógica, social media presence, written records of activities and games, and a clearer identity. He stopped working at the original 2024 school.

### Two different schools

He taught at two new schools in 2025 and was the sole in-person instructor. The historical notes describe:

- Approximately a full day and a half of teaching across them.
- Coordination with school staff and dedicated rooms.
- Groups of six students per grade per session, rotating through periods.
- One school covering 4th–6th grade and the other 1st–5th grade.

The approach was more organized than in 2024. He continued creating activities, and classes ran until the end of 2025. Group sizes and grade ranges describe historical arrangements, not today's digital audience or a total reach count.

### The website suggestion

Daqui Pra Fora shared JcL with its community, and students reached out to help. One asked whether the project had a website. That question unintentionally supplied the idea near the start of the second year of the in-person project, when it already had a name and identity.

The idea was adapting in-person activities into a digital platform. Bruno considers it one of the best things that happened to him because it greatly expanded the project's possible future directions.

Development began in early 2025. He first learned TypeScript and React, built basic features, and gradually became more capable. His later move toward agents writing nearly all code is described in [founder-context.md](founder-context.md).

### MVP and launch

The MVP target included six entries, an About page, a Contact page, and teacher resources:

1. Stop Matemático.
2. Caça Coroa.
3. Caça Soma.
4. Guerra Matemática.
5. Super Jogo da Velha, using the existing Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe concept.
6. Cubo Mágico, central to the initial identity.

The notes describe the other entries as Bruno's in-person activities adapted for digital use. This concerns authorship of activities, not a claim that every underlying mechanic was invented from scratch. Cube activities connected visual structures to math, such as using face squares to explore multiplication. Some adaptations were small, others substantial.

More games and activities existed in project files than could be implemented initially because of time constraints. The website launched in late 2025.

## Transition in 2026

Bruno started college in 2026 and no longer had time for the previous teaching schedule. Classes ended after 2025. There is no confirmed commitment to restart; the earlier prediction that this format probably would not return was not a permanent decision.

JcL continued through development, team collaboration, and educator outreach after the applications. Attachment and ownership became reasons to continue beyond the original motivation.

By October 12, 2026, Bruno had contacted a coordinator at his old school and presented the website to a teacher. He believes she tried it a few times, supported by local analytics activity on several days, but does not know whether use will continue. This is separate from Bruno personally teaching in schools and does not establish regular digital adoption or measured educational outcomes.
